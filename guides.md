## How Much Concrete Do I Need? — `/concrete/how-much-concrete-do-i-need`

- Multiply length × width × thickness — all three in feet — to get cubic feet, then divide by 27 to get cubic yards.
- A 10 × 10 ft slab at 4 in thick is 10 × 10 × 0.333 = 33.33 cubic feet, which is 33.33 ÷ 27 = 1.23 cubic yards.
- At 80 lb per bag with a 0.6 cubic foot yield, that is 56 bags before any waste allowance (59 at 5%).
- Thickness conversion: 3 in → 0.25 ft; 4 in → 0.333; 5 in → 0.417; 6 in → 0.5; 8 in → 0.667; 12 in → 1.0.
- Bag yields: 40 lb 0.30 cu ft (90 bags/yd³); 50 lb 0.375 (72); 60 lb 0.45 (60); 80 lb 0.60 (45).
- Common slabs at 4 in: 4 × 8 ft → 10.7 cu ft / 0.40 yd³ / 18 bags; 10 × 10 ft → 33.3 / 1.23 / 56; 12 × 12 ft → 48.0 / 1.78 / 80; 20 × 20 ft → 133.3 / 4.94 / 222; 24 × 24 ft → 192.0 / 7.11 / 320.
- A 20 ft footing at 24 in wide and 12 in deep is 20 × 2 × 1 = 40 cubic feet, or 1.48 cubic yards.
- A 10 in diameter hole, 30 in deep, around a 4 × 4 post takes about 1.1 cubic feet, which is 2 bags of 80 lb.
- On a 10 × 10 slab at 4 in that is 1.23 cubic yards net, so order 1.29 — 1.5 yards at most plants — or 59 bags of 80 lb instead of 56.
- One cubic yard of concrete weighs roughly 4,000 lb.
- 3 m × 3 m × 0.1 m = 0.9 m³. One cubic metre is about 1.31 cubic yards.
- Metric bagged concrete is sold as 20 kg or 25 kg; counted at 0.00936 m³ and 0.0117 m³ per bag (about 107 and 85 bags per cubic metre).
- FAQ: 10 × 10 slab at 4 in = 33.33 cu ft / 1.23 yd³ / 56 bags of 80 lb, 75 of 60 lb, 112 of 40 lb before waste; with 5% buy 59 / 78 / 117.
- FAQ: 80 lb bag ≈ 0.6 cu ft, 60 lb 0.45, 50 lb 0.375, 40 lb 0.30.
- FAQ: about 45 bags of 80 lb make a cubic yard (27 ÷ 0.6 = 45).
- FAQ: a cubic yard covers 81 sq ft at 4 in, 108 sq ft at 3 in, 54 sq ft at 6 in.
- FAQ: a 24 × 24 slab at 4 in = 192 cu ft / 7.11 yd³; 10.7 yd³ at 6 in.

## How Much Is a Yard of Concrete? — `/concrete/how-much-is-a-yard-of-concrete`

- A cubic yard is 27 cubic feet of concrete weighing about 4,000 lb.
- A 4,000 PSI mix for a driveway costs more per yard than a 2,500 PSI mix for a shed base.
- One cubic yard covers 81 sq ft at 4 in thick, 108 sq ft at 3 in, 54 sq ft at 6 in. Coverage = 27 ÷ thickness in feet.
- A 20 × 20 slab needs about five yards at 4 in.
- Concrete has to be placed within about 90 minutes of mixing.
- A yard at $165 covers 81 sq ft at 4 in, which is about $2.04 per sq ft of material.
- Slab costs at an example $165/yd: 10 × 10 ft → 1.23 yd³ / $203; 12 × 12 ft → 1.78 / $294; 20 × 20 ft → 4.94 / $815; 24 × 24 ft → 7.11 / $1,173.
- Bags cost roughly two to three times more per cubic yard than ready-mix; a full yard is 45 bags of 80 lb.
- Waiting time after the first 5–10 minutes on site can appear on the invoice.
- FAQ: 10 × 10 slab needs 1.23 cubic yards at 4 in, 1.85 at 6 in; order the next quarter-yard up.

## How Long Does Concrete Take to Cure? — `/concrete/how-long-does-concrete-take-to-cure`

- Initial set 2–4 hours; final set 8–24 hours; light foot traffic 24–48 hours.
- Forms off vertical faces 24 hours; ~70% of design strength at 7 days; forms off slab edges 7 days; full design strength 28 days.
- The 28-day figure comes from the standard strength test; concrete keeps gaining strength after 28 days, slowly, over months.
- Foot traffic is safe once the surface takes no footprint, usually 24 to 48 hours in mild weather.
- Seven days is the usual threshold for a car, and 28 days for anything heavy. A 4-inch patio slab is not a driveway at any age.
- 50–70°F (10–21°C): normal timeline. Above 85°F (29°C): sets faster but cracks more. 40–50°F (4–10°C): roughly doubles the wait for each stage. Below 40°F (4°C): reaction nearly stops.
- Cover or mist during the first 24 hours. Water curing for 7 days is the standard every method is measured against.
- FAQ: walk in 24 to 48 hours; drive at about 7 days for a car; full strength 28 days. A driveway pour should be 5 to 6 inches thick.
- FAQ: below 40°F (4°C) the reaction almost stops and freezing water will damage the mix.

## Concrete Mix Ratio Chart — `/concrete/mix-ratio`

- 1 : 2 : 3 → ~3,500–4,000 PSI → slabs, patios, walkways, general.
- 1 : 2 : 4 → ~3,000 PSI → general purpose, shed bases.
- 1 : 3 : 3 → ~2,500–3,000 PSI → footings, mass fill, non-structural.
- 1 : 1.5 : 3 → ~4,500 PSI → higher-strength slabs, garage floors.
- 1 : 3 : 6 → ~1,500–2,000 PSI → fill, kerb backing, not structural.
- Around 0.5 by weight is the usual water target — roughly 1 part water to 2 parts cement by weight.
- An 80 lb bag typically takes around 3 quarts (about 6 pints) of water; a 60 lb bag about 2.5 quarts.
- Most small mixers take a half bag to a full 80 lb bag per batch; concrete starts setting in about 30 minutes.
- FAQ: 1:2:3 for slabs at roughly 3,500–4,000 PSI; 1:3:3 for footings and non-structural fill.
- FAQ: about 30 minutes before mixed concrete is unusable, less in hot weather.
